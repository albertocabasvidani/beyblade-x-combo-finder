Wizard Rod is the second most-played blade in the competitive Beyblade X meta, behind only [Shark Scale](/parts/shark-scale/) by number of aggregated events: 3,188 tournament placements and 1,377 wins spread over 193 distinct combos. These are the numbers of a reference stamina blade, not a supporting player: with 60 stamina out of 100 (against 15 attack and 25 defense), Wizard Rod is built to win by exhaustion, and it does so better than almost anything else in the format. Debuted in the UX-03 booster on March 30, 2024, it also arrived in the Hasbro Starter Packs G1537 and G1538 and in Random Booster Vol. 4 (BX-35), which makes it one of the easiest parts to get for anyone who wants to enter a tournament with a competitive stamina build.

## Why the 1-60 Hexa build dominates

The [Wizard Rod 1-60 Hexa](/combos/wizard-rod-1-60-hexa/) combination is not just the best build of the blade: it is combo #1 of the entire all-time WBO ranking, with 3087 WBO points and 900 wins according to that ranking. The 9.62/10 score on this site puts it above any other combo ever rated, and the tournament numbers confirm it: 1,279 events, 709 wins, 2,274 top cut appearances. A 14% meta share over the last month means that more than one in seven players in tournaments is playing it right now, not just that it was played in the past.

The figure that weighs the most is the 2025 World Championship: both finalists, gold and silver, used it. This is not a niche combo that works in a favorable bracket: it is the choice of those who reach the world final. And the most recent signal comes from September 19, 2026, when at the event "Mata Nui's X Extravaganza 2" it finished 1st, 2nd and 3rd in the same tournament on the Xtreme stadium. When a single combo takes the whole podium, it is no longer a question of player skill: it is the combo that is dictating the format.

## The weak point: Cobalt Dragoon

Wizard Rod 1-60 Hexa wins on pure stamina, but there is a blade that beats it on exactly that ground, and not by playing the same game. [Cobalt Dragoon 5-60 Elevate](/combos/cobalt-dragoon-5-60-elevate/) is a left-spin stealer: it does not outlast Wizard Rod, it steals its spin through erosion. It is a distinction that matters, because it means that against this specific build Wizard Rod's raw stamina is not enough: the matchup is decided by a different mechanism from the one the combo is optimized for.

With a score of 8.68, Cobalt Dragoon 5-60 Elevate is not at the level of Wizard Rod 1-60 Hexa in overall rating, but its growth is the figure to watch: from 8 to 24 placements between July and August 2026, almost tripled. It is no longer a niche tech pick: it is an answer that players are adopting more and more, specifically because they know what it does against the format's dominant combo.

## Against Shark Scale: stamina versus attack

The most natural comparison in the meta remains with [Shark Scale](/parts/shark-scale/), the most-played attack blade of all. In the [1-70 Low Rush](/combos/shark-scale-1-70-low-rush/) build, score 8.93, it is combo #2 of the entire all-time WBO ranking, behind only Wizard Rod 1-60 Hexa. It was used by the silver medalist (Balya) at the 2025 World Championship, the same tournament in which Wizard Rod took both spots on the top step of the podium.

These are two opposite philosophies coexisting at the top of the same meta: Shark Scale aims for the KO or the burst before the match is decided by endurance, Wizard Rod plays the long game and lets the opponent wear themselves out first. The fact that both top-2 all-time combos represent radically different styles says something about the current format: there is not one single way to win in tournaments, but two archetypes contending for the top, and the choice between the two depends on how you want to handle the matchup rather than on an absolute statistical advantage of one style over the other.

## The Narrow variant and where to find it

There is also Wizard Rod 1-60 Narrow: score 4.81, much weaker than the Hexa build, with growth from 2 to 18 placements in August that indicates some experimental interest but no real threat to the main build's dominance. It is not worth going into further: anyone playing Wizard Rod in tournaments plays Hexa.

For those who still need to get the part, the most direct entry point remains the [UX-03](/buy/UX-03/) booster, the set that introduced it with the 5-70DB stock, the same ratchet/bit combination reissued in the G1537 Starter Pack. Alternatively, Random Booster Vol. 4 (BX-35) and the G1538 Starter Pack with the 1-60R stock offer other ways to get to the same blade.
