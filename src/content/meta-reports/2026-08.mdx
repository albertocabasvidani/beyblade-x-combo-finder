August 2026 produced 1,203 tournament placements across the events we track — enough volume to separate a real shift from a single event's noise. Comparing every combo's placement count against July, the same month one year into competitive Beyblade X's current era, gives a clean before/after: who's climbing, who's fading, and by how much.

## The month's biggest riser: Cobalt Dragoon, not Wizard Rod

The largest single jump belongs to [Cobalt Dragoon 5-60 Elevate](/combos/cobalt-dragoon-5-60-elevate/): 8 placements in July, 24 in August — tripled in a month. That's not the kind of move a single lucky bracket produces; it's the shape of adoption, players picking up a specific tool because they know what it's for. Cobalt Dragoon is a left-spin stealer, and the tool it answers is the format's dominant combo.

That combo is [Wizard Rod 1-60 Hexa](/parts/wizard-rod/), still the #1 combo on the WBO all-time list and the highest-scoring build on this site. It didn't fall — it climbed too, from 100 to 125 placements. Both of the format's most important combos grew in the same month, which is less a contradiction than a sign of what's happening: the more Wizard Rod 1-60 Hexa dominates, the more players reach for the one answer that reliably beats it in pure endurance.

## Shark Scale's two winning ratchets both grew

[Shark Scale](/parts/shark-scale/)'s two tournament-proven builds moved in the same direction as each other, and both up. [Shark Scale 1-70 Low Rush](/combos/shark-scale-1-70-low-rush/) went from 20 to 32 placements; Shark Scale 3-60 Low Rush from 15 to 27. Two different ratchets on the same attack blade, both gaining roughly 60% in a single month — not noise on small numbers, a trend moving the same way on two distinct combos built around the same part.

A third rising combo worth a mention: Silver Wolf 9-60 Free Ball climbed from 23 to 39 placements, one of the larger absolute gains of the month. It doesn't have a dedicated write-up on this site yet, but the number is real and worth watching into September.

Two smaller builds moved enough to flag without over-reading them. Wizard Rod 1-60 Narrow — a much weaker variant of the site's top-scoring blade, well below the Hexa build in every metric — went from 2 to 18 placements. Glory Valkyrie Kick moved from 2 to 12. Both are small-sample jumps off a low base, the kind of move that can just as easily be a one-off local pod as the start of something real; they're noted here so next month's report can check whether either one held.

## What faded

Not every attack build shared in the growth. Meteor Dragoon 7-60 Level dropped from 22 to 13 placements — the largest single fall of the month. Two older Aero Pegasus and Shark Scale variants lost ground too: Shark Scale 1-60 Low Rush fell from 12 to 8, and two low-count Aero Pegasus builds (1-50 Low Rush, 1-70 Kick) each lost a handful of placements from an already small base.

The Shark Scale case is worth pulling apart from the others: it's not that Shark Scale is losing ground overall — the blade's two current winning builds (1-70 and 3-60 Low Rush) both grew, as noted above. It's specifically the older 1-60 ratchet variant that's fading while its own blade's other configurations rise. That's a meta shifting inside a single part, not a part falling out of the meta.

## What to watch into September

Three threads carry into next month's report: whether Cobalt Dragoon 5-60 Elevate's growth continues as a durable answer to Wizard Rod or plateaus as a counter-pick that gets played around; whether Shark Scale's shift away from the 1-60 ratchet toward 1-70/3-60 keeps going; and whether Silver Wolf 9-60 Free Ball's jump turns into a write-up-worthy trend or fades back down. September's own numbers, when the month closes, will settle at least the first two.
