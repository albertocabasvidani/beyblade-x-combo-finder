Shark Scale 1-70 Low Rush is the aggressive soul of [Shark Scale](/parts/shark-scale/): the most played blade in the whole game, here mounted on a setup that leaves no room for compromise. The 1-70 ratchet extends the contact radius compared with the shorter variants, giving the blade more surface to intercept the opponent on every revolution. The Low Rush bit completes the picture by pushing the combo into a low, insistent trajectory, designed to pile up hits rather than wait for a single opening. The blade's stat profile (atk70/def15/sta15) already says it all: this isn't about endurance, it's about constant pressure until a KO or until the opponent is knocked out of the stadium.

## Why it works

The numbers describe a combo that has moved past the experimentation phase and entered the set of first-choice picks. Score 8.93/10, with a breakdown that weighs heavily on pure performance (0.962) rather than presence (0.832) or corroboration (0.783) — the sign of a combo that wins when it plays, even if it isn't yet as ubiquitous as the top of the class. 624 placements recorded, 286 wins, 810 top cut appearances: an enormous volume of data for an attack build, a category that usually produces less consistency than stamina or balanced builds.

The most recent data point is the double podium of September 19, 2026 at the Dallas Beyblade Association - The Beycon Blitz - Standard#180: first and second place in the same tournament, 17 players, the same combo in the hands of two different players. It isn't an isolated hit, it's confirmation that the setup doesn't depend on a single pilot's skill to perform. And there is the highest showcase possible: Balya took it to the silver medal at the 2025 World Championship, the certification that many combos that only shine on local circuits lack. In the separate WBO all-time ranking it is #2 overall, with 1,400 points and 276 wins according to that scoring system — a placement that puts Shark Scale 1-70 Low Rush just below [Wizard Rod 1-60 Hexa](/combos/wizard-rod-1-60-hexa/), the top of the ranking.

## Why it rose in August

The August 2026 trend is clear: from 20 to 32 placements compared with July, growth of 60% in a month. With 3.2% meta share in the last month and confirmed use on Xtreme and Infinity stadiums, the combo has carved out a stable place among the top-tier attack picks. The push is consistent with the international exposure: a result at Worlds 2025 doesn't produce an immediate effect, but it feeds adoption in the following months as players replicate what worked at the highest level. September's double podium is the most recent snapshot of this trajectory, not an episode disconnected from it.

## Its limit

The combo that beats it most often is, not by chance, the one at the top of the ranking: [Wizard Rod 1-60 Hexa](/combos/wizard-rod-1-60-hexa/), score 9.62. It is a structural problem, not an execution one: Wizard Rod 1-60 Hexa is a pure stamina build, designed to absorb and withstand pressure rather than clash with it. Shark Scale 1-70 Low Rush lives on speed and repeated hits, but against an opponent that takes the hits and waits, that same speed stops being a decisive advantage: if the attack doesn't close the match before the opponent's endurance makes itself felt, time works against the attacker. It is the archetypal attack-versus-stamina clash, and here stamina wins more often — the same imbalance you see when comparing with hybrid builds like [Cobalt Dragoon 5-60 Elevate](/combos/cobalt-dragoon-5-60-elevate/), where a more balanced profile reduces exposure to this kind of matchup.
