The stock ratchet 5-70 and bit DB (Down Ball) define a stamina configuration, consistent with Wizard Rod's raw stats (atk15/def25/sta60): built to spin for a long time, not to win more. In the box you get the blade, the 5-70 ratchet and the DB bit — nothing else.

## What's in the box

UX-03 is a single Booster: it contains the [Wizard Rod](/parts/wizard-rod/) blade, the 5-70 ratchet and the DB (Down Ball) bit, assembled as the stock configuration. That is everything that comes in the package — no additional parts, no second blade.

The part that matters is the blade. Wizard Rod is the second most used blade in tournaments across the whole game: 3,188 aggregate placements, 1,377 wins, 193 distinct combos built around it. A part you see everywhere, in very different configurations.

## Why the stock build is not the one that wins

It has to be said plainly here: 5-70 DB is not the build that brings home results. It's an honest stamina configuration, but the number of distinct combos (193) that use Wizard Rod says that most of its value is played out on other ratchets and other bits.

The build that actually dominates is [Wizard Rod 1-60 Hexa](/combos/wizard-rod-1-60-hexa/) — ratchet 1-60, bit Hexa. It's not just one variant among many: it is the number 1 combo in the entire all-time WBO ranking, with a score of 9.62/10, 3,087 aggregate points and 900 wins according to that ranking. In the recent data: 1,279 tournament events, 709 wins, used by both finalists (gold and silver) of the World Championship 2025, and on September 19, 2026 it took a full podium — 1st, 2nd and 3rd place — at the Mata Nui's X Extravaganza 2 event.

The gap between "5-70 DB" and "1-60 Hexa" is not cosmetic: they are two different ratchets and two different bits, both to be sourced elsewhere. Buying UX-03 means buying the blade — the piece that stays identical in both builds — not the ready-to-use combo. The 1-60 ratchet and the Hexa bit are not in the box and have to be taken from other sets.

## The alternatives: G1537, G1538, BX-35

There are two other Hasbro routes to the same blade, and they are not equivalent to each other.

**G1537** (Starter Pack) ships with the same stock 5-70 DB as UX-03: same situation, no advantage in taking that one over this one. If you're choosing only on the stock configuration, the two behave identically.

**G1538** (Starter Pack) is different: it ships with the stock 1-60R, meaning it already has the right 1-60 ratchet — the one from the winning build — mounted as standard. The bit is "R", not Hexa, so it's not the identical champion combo, but it's still closer to the build that matters than UX-03 or G1537. If the goal is to get to the 1-60 without hunting for it elsewhere, G1538 saves you one part to source.

**BX-35** (Random Booster Vol. 4) is another way to get the blade, but it's a surprise booster: you don't know what comes out before opening it, so it's a less predictable path if the goal is specifically Wizard Rod.

## The honest verdict

UX-03 makes sense if what you're looking for is the Wizard Rod blade itself, not a build ready to win out of the box. It's the most used piece of the set, and from there you can build any of the 193 documented combos — including the champion one — but the ratchet and bit that complete the tournament build you have to find elsewhere.

If instead you want to get closer to the winning configuration without buying extra parts, G1538 with the stock 1-60R is the most sensible choice among the alternatives listed: it already gives you the right ratchet, even if not the Hexa bit. UX-03 remains the direct choice only if the blade is the only thing you care about, and you already know where to get the rest.
